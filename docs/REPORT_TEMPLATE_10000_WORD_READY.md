## Abstract

This study tests whether LLM judges systematically prefer LLM-generated training plans over programmatically generated controls when deterministic structural plan quality is controlled. The design compares matched pairs consisting of one LLM-source plan and one programmatic plan from the same fixture cell and with similar source-neutral structural quality. The current Qwen/Gemma study uses Qwen and Gemma source plans, programmatic controls with a shared explainer for narrative fields, and four local Qwen/Gemma judge models. The full evaluation requires at least 250 matched pairs, five repeated runs, and both AB and BA positions, yielding at least 10,000 pairwise judgments. Primary outcomes estimate the LLM-source win rate overall and by judge model, judge family, source family, order, score gap, and self-family match where defined. ## 1. Introduction

Large language models are increasingly used as evaluators of outputs produced by other language models. This creates a methodological risk: if an LLM judge prefers stylistic, rhetorical, or family-specific artifacts of LLM generation, measured quality may reflect source identity rather than task-relevant merit. This risk is especially important in applied domains where deterministic quality can be partially specified but presentation quality may remain variable.

This project studies that risk in the domain of trail-running training plans. The central question is whether LLM judges prefer LLM-generated training plans over programmatically generated plans when the two are matched on deterministic structural quality. The design intentionally separates structural quality from presentation quality. Structural quality includes features such as plan length, weekly duration, rest days, hard-day count, long-run presence, quality-session presence, and alignment with fixture-level constraints. Presentation quality includes title wording, workout prose, purpose prose, explanation richness, citations, claim attributions, and data-note verbosity.

The study does not assume self-preference. It treats self-family preference as a hypothesis: Qwen judges may or may not prefer Qwen-source plans, and Gemma judges may or may not prefer Gemma-source plans. Such an analysis is interpretable only where both source plans and judge models from the same family are present.

## 2. Research questions

RQ1: Controlling for deterministic structural plan quality, do LLM judges prefer LLM-source plans over programmatic controls more often than chance?

RQ2: Does the preference vary by judge model, judge family, or model size?

RQ3: Is there evidence of self-family preference, defined as greater preference for plans generated by the judge's own family, where source and judge families overlap?

RQ4: Are preference patterns concentrated in presentation-adjacent rubrics such as explanation quality, or do they also appear in structure-adjacent rubrics such as plan coherence?

RQ5: Are results robust to AB/BA order, score-gap sensitivity, fixture-cell subgrouping, and style-leakage audits?

## 3. Hypotheses

H1: The LLM-source win rate will differ from 0.5 after structural matching.

H2: The win rate will vary by judge family and model size.

H3: Self-family match may increase the probability that the judge selects the LLM-source plan, but this hypothesis is tested only for Qwen and Gemma intersections represented in the data.

H4: If preference is driven partly by presentation style, effects may be larger for explanation-quality judgments than for plan-coherence judgments.

## 12. Limitations and threats to validity

Equal structural score is not full equality. A structural score compresses many dimensions into a single number and can miss qualitative differences in training logic. Matching reduces but does not eliminate confounding.

Presentation leakage remains possible. Even with masking and style audits, LLM-source and programmatic plans may differ in subtle wording, sequencing, or explanation style. Programmatic plans may use LLM-written explanation fields, which reduces empty-text artifacts but introduces its own ambiguity: the programmatic arm is not prose-free.

Style normalization can remove real-world variation. If LLM-generated plans are genuinely more usable because of prose quality, masking or truncation may understate real-world preference. Conversely, failing to normalize presentation can overstate structural preference.

Local model constraints may affect external validity. The results apply most directly to the Qwen/Gemma local judge set and the trail-running training-plan domain. They should not be generalized to all LLM judges or all evaluation tasks without replication.

Judge contamination or family familiarity is possible. A judge may recognize family-specific style or training distributions. Self-family analysis can identify patterns but cannot by itself prove mechanism.

Temperature sensitivity must be interpreted as sensitivity, not a replacement primary outcome, unless designated before analysis.
